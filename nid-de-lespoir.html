OUR IDENTITY

“NEST OF HOPE positions itself as a centre for welcoming, reintegrating into education and providing comprehensive support for children who have dropped out of school and those of school age.

  • Name: NID DE L’ESPOIR
  • Address: Fokontany ANDRANOMENA, Betsinjaka Commune, Tulear II District, Atsimo Andrefana Region, Madagascar
  • Status: Non-profit association under Malagasy law
  • Type: Religious centre incorporating a school.
  • Target audience: Children and adolescents aged 6 to 17 who have dropped out of school or in aged to be schooled.
  • Initial capacity (2025-2026): 30 pupils, i.e. one class at the first level.
  • Target capacity in 5 years: 120 pupils.
  • Duration of the cycle: Accelerated primary and secondary programme over 7 years.

OUR STORY

The NID DE L’ESPOIR project was born in 2022 from a simple observation: too many children in our community are deprived of their fundamental right to education. Some have never been to school, others have dropped out too early due to lack of resources or difficult family situations.

Faced with this reality, we opened a school that welcomes these ‘forgotten’ children, with a tailored programme to give them back the chance to dream, learn and grow. From the very first months, we saw transformations: eyes full of life, hands eager to write their future.

Today, building on this first step, we want to go further: to open a secondary school with a condensed two-year programme to support older young people in continuing their education, with a solid foundation and caring supervision.

Le Nid de l'Espoir is more than just a place of learning. It is a springboard for life, a space where dreams, dignity and confidence in the future are reborn.

OUR MISSIONS

The School Reintegration Center "Nid de l’Espoir works to give vulnerable children a second chance through holistic care based on Christian values. Our mission is built around five key areas:

ACCELERATED SCHOOLING

Offer an adapted educational program to help children who have dropped out or are behind in school quickly regain their academic level and restore their dignity as students.

EXTRACURRICULAR ACTIVITIES

Encourage personal development through sports, arts, educational games, environmental awareness, and community life.

SCHOOL CANTEEN

Provide a balanced daily meal to ensure pupils' health, wellbeing and concentration.

PSYCHOSOCIAL AND SPIRITUAL SUPPORT

Ensure emotional and behavioral support through caring supervision, listening sessions, and tailored assistance. The goal is to promote self-confidence and the discovery of their identity in Christ.

THE CONDENSED PROGRAMME

The condensed curriculum has been introduced because children at risk of dropping out of school are already at a more advanced age compared to their peers. It enables them to catch up on essential learning in a short space of time, in order to facilitate their reintegration into the education system. Tailored to their level and pace, it restores their confidence, reinforces their fundamental skills and increases their chances of continuing their education in the long term.

The primary school years are grouped into four levels, with the curriculum focusing on reading and comprehension, writing and mathematics. Furthermore, the four years of lower secondary education will be condensed into a three-year period, and upon obtaining the Brevet certificate, pupils are expected to reintegrate into the mainstream education system.

  • Development of a school curriculum drawn up by teachers and education specialists.
  • Support with learning spoken languages (French and English) and basic IT skills.
  • Use of visual, playful and hands-on materials to facilitate learning.
  • Ongoing teacher training before and during the school year, and a monthly debriefing session to evaluate the system.

ACCELERATED SCHOOLING PERFORMANCE INDICATORS

  • 90 children enrolled over a three-year period, with an exam pass rate of 90–100 per cent
  • 30 pupils supported each year prior to moving on to secondary school or a vocational training programme.
  • Establishment of a vocational training programme within the centre after five years.

FROM A TEMPORARY SPACE TO A PERMANENT CENTRE

The Today – Starting with what we have

The project began with limited resources. To give children who have dropped out of school a second chance, we adapted our own home into a temporary learning and welcoming space. This has enabled us to start the programme and respond to an urgent need, despite the limitations of our current facilities.

Within 5 years – Building a dedicated Centre

Our main priority for the next five years is to build dedicated facilities for the Centre. We want to move from our current adapted space to a permanent, safe and suitable educational environment designed specifically for children. The new Centre will provide appropriate classrooms, spaces for study and activities, and a welcoming environment where children can learn, grow and regain confidence.

Within 5–10 years – Building a sustainable and self-supporting Centre

Once the Centre has its own facilities, our long-term goal will be to strengthen its sustainability through the development of agriculture and livestock farming. These activities will help contribute to the Centre’s operating needs and gradually reduce its dependence on external support. In this way, the construction of the Centre will not simply provide a building, but become the foundation for a lasting educational project capable of supporting vulnerable children for many years to come.
